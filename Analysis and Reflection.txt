Analysis:

The goal of this project was to determine whether two features - total hours played and self-reported experience level - could predict whether a Minecraft player subscribed to the community newsletter. Because the data came from a custom Minecraft server used in a university setting, the aim was to understand not only the predictive relationship but also the implications for engagement patterns and future data collection.

Wrangling data:
The group worked on linking the two datasets (sessions.csv & players.csv) together as one dataset reports experience level, while the other dataset reports number of hours played. Although players.csv also reports number of hours played, sessions.csv was used as it provided more information regarding play time. As both datasets have one variable in common (hashedEmail), they were combined and merged into one dataset: players_data_tidy. In it showed the columns: experience, subscribe, and played_hours. 

As KNNs require numerical values, experience values were changed from categorical variables to numerical variables ranging from 1-5 based on the five categories of experience (Beginner, Amateur, Regular, Veteran, Pro). Furthermore, timestamps used in sessions.csv were converted into DateTime format for a more effective analysis.

Model Preparation and Building:
K-Nearest Neighbors Classification was used in this project as it was suited for this type of problem. Since it was used to calculate distance, two numerical non-linear variables were used to predict subscription status of the player. Model performance was evaluated with accuracy, precision, and recall, with accuracy being the main evaluator. The training dataset and testing dataset was split 80/20 and standardization was used to prevent the model from favouring one variable over the other.

In building the model, the group opted for a 10-cross fold - where the model gets trained on 9 parts of the data and gets tested on 1 - rather than a 5-cross fold as it was seen to have higher accuracy results. The group used GridSearchCV to automatically test many versions of the KNN model, each with a different number of neighbors, and checked which one gave the best prediction accuracy. After GridSearchCV tested all the different values of n_neighbors, it produced a table of results. From that table, the number of neighbors tested, the average accuracy for each number of neighbors (mean_test_score), and the std_mean_score was pulled out. But, GridSearchCV does not directly give you the standard error. Instead, it gives the standard deviation (std_test_score). To convert standard deviation to standard error, the std_test_score was divided by the square root of the amount of folds (10). The column std_test_score was renamed into sem_test_score.

A plot showing accuracy vs number of K was plotted. From there, it was determined that the best K would be 1 as it showed the highest accuracy in the plot. THe model was then retrained to have n_neighbors_ = 1. After all the retraining, the accuracy of the model came out to be 52.5%. Overall, the model’s performance is quite poor, as an accuracy of 52.5% is only slightly better than random guessing. This indicates that the features used - experience level and hours played - do not provide meaningful information for distinguishing subscribers from non-subscribers. Despite optimizing K and evaluating multiple values through cross-validation, the KNN model was unable to detect any reliable patterns in the data. However, the project was done extremely well and opened new doors for future studies regarding this topic.
